The method

Olympiad problems are not harder school maths. They reward a small set of techniques that school rarely teaches. The sprint teaches them one at a time and then mixes them.

Day 1

Diagnose

A 25-problem mixed test maps what the child already knows. Mistakes become the schedule.

Days 2–25

A technique a day

Bar models, working backwards, differences tables, worst case, counting figures… Ten problems each, a hint only after an honest attempt, and a picture-based explanation for every technique.

Every day

Error journal

Every wrong answer is saved with its explanation and comes back until it is solved. The weakest topic is always on top.

Days 20, 25, 27

Mock exams

25 problems, 90 minutes, real scoring and penalties. Children learn when to skip and when never to leave a blank: tactics worth more points than an extra topic.

Evidence so far

Case study: two Grade 2 pupils went through the sprint before an international maths olympiad (SASMO). One won gold, the other silver.

Two children are a case, not a study. That is why the product now measures itself: a diagnostic test before the sprint and a parallel test after it, per-topic growth, time per problem, and a printable report for parents and teachers. The first school pilot (60 children, with a control group) is designed to turn the case into data.
