OlympiadSprint
A 28-day maths olympiad programme for primary schools · EN / AR / RU

The problem

Maths olympiads reward a handful of techniques that school rarely teaches: bar models, working backwards, difference tables, worst-case reasoning, counting figures. Strong pupils lose points not on knowledge but on technique and exam tactics. Coaching is expensive and uneven, and most practice material is a pile of papers without structure or feedback.

The method

  • Diagnose on day 1 with a parallel-form test.
  • One technique a day, ten problems in rising difficulty, a hint only after an honest try, and a picture-based explanation for each technique.
  • Error journal: every mistake returns until it is solved; the weakest topic is on top.
  • Three timed mock exams under real scoring rules, including penalties and tactics.
  • Re-test after the sprint with the parallel form and report growth per topic.

Evidence so far

Two Grade 2 pupils took the sprint before an international olympiad (SASMO): one gold, one silver.

A case, not a study. The product now measures itself (pre/post parallel forms, per-topic growth, time per problem) so that the pilot below produces data, not anecdotes.

What exists today

  • 660 problems for Grades 2 and 3 across 30 topics, each with a hint and a worked solution.
  • 49 techniques explained with diagrams (“I don't get it — explain”).
  • Seven full past olympiad papers with their own scoring rules.
  • Pre/post assessment with counterbalanced A/B forms and a printable progress report.
  • Accounts with pseudonymous child profiles, cloud sync and a teacher dashboard.
  • Runs offline: in a browser, as a single file, or as an Android app.
  • English interface; Russian content with English demo lessons.
